# Walkthrough script and shot list

One real session on https://nativeproof.vercel.app (October 29, 2026), live mode only, recorded with the judging account. Each shot lists what is on screen, the voiceover, the length of the real recording, and the playback speed used in the video. The voiceover is synthetic (Microsoft Edge text to speech, `en-US-AndrewMultilingualNeural`).

## 1. Nativeproof

*A real session on nativeproof.vercel.app with live Qloo data and live models*

- **On screen:** title card
- **Voiceover:** This is Nativeproof, a research workspace that tests how a campaign may land in another culture before money is spent on it. Everything in this video is one real session on the deployed app, recorded on October twenty-ninth: live Qloo cultural data, live language models, and synthetic participants. The participants are generated by AI. They are not real people, and the app labels them that way everywhere.

## 3. Project, brand kit and campaign

*Every edit creates a new content version*

- **Recording:** `02-project.mp4`
- **Real time:** 1 min 43 s; shown at 3.97x
- **Voiceover:** We create a live project for a fictional brand, Trailhead Coffee, launching canned cold brew for weekend hikers in Japan. The brand kit records the voice, terms that must stay verbatim, and terms that are not allowed. Then we add the campaign copy and the cultural references to investigate. Every edit creates a new content version, so each later result names exactly which copy it tested.

## 4. Atlas: Qloo evidence

*Ambiguous matches are confirmed by a person, never guessed*

- **Recording:** `03-atlas.mp4`
- **Real time:** 6 min 46 s; shown at 10.58x
- **Voiceover:** Atlas resolves the campaign's concepts against Qloo. In this live run, all five terms came back ambiguous. Qloo holds several tags named Hiking, for example as a hobby, an activity and a lifestyle. Nativeproof does not guess. It lists the candidates, and we confirm one with Use this. Each choice re-runs discovery, and each run took between thirty and sixty-six seconds. With all five confirmed, ten signals resolved. The market brief separates source data from model interpretation, and its risks state plainly that affinity data does not prove demand.

## 5. Quick rehearsal

*A study with research questions, grounded in discovery evidence*

- **Recording:** `04-audience-setup.mp4`
- **Real time:** 3 min 7 s; shown at 8.12x
- **Voiceover:** A population study builds on a study, so we create one with two research questions and run the quick rehearsal. It generated twelve synthetic reactions grounded in ten discovery evidence records, in fifty-six seconds. It raised something we had not considered: several reactions were unsure how hikers would carry and dispose of the can.

## 6. Population study: 100 participants

*Background run with checkpoints, cancel and resume*

- **Recording:** `05-population-start.mp4`
- **Real time:** 1 min 45 s; shown at 4.11x
- **Voiceover:** Now the main study. We build a new synthetic population of one hundred participants for Japan at standard interview depth, and we upload the ad creative, so participants react to what a vision model actually observes in the image. The study runs in the background and saves checkpoints. It can be cancelled or resumed, and a server restart marks it resumable instead of losing the work.

## 7. Population results

*Deterministic tallies of stored interviews*

- **Recording:** `06-population-results.mp4`
- **Real time:** 3 min 1 s; shown at 4.41x
- **Voiceover:** The study finished in two minutes and twenty seconds, and all one hundred interviews completed. Seventy-one percent were positive, twelve mixed and seventeen neutral, with none negative or confused. The participants come from four groups built from the Qloo evidence, twenty-five in each. Every number is a tally of stored interviews, not a model's estimate. The reaction graph links campaign elements, groups, reactions and themes. The landscape maps the groups to their Qloo interests. Every interview is labeled as AI-generated, and the recommendations name their own gaps, such as the absence of negative voices.

## 8. Multi-round social simulation

*Stance changes with reasons and the posts that caused them*

- **Recording:** `07-social-simulation.mp4`
- **Real time:** 2 min 45 s; shown at 5.51x
- **Voiceover:** Next, a three round social simulation. In each round, twenty percent of the participants read a feed of five peer posts, then post, comment, share or ignore. It completed in seventy seconds, with nine, four and eight stance changes across the rounds. Each post records the stance before and after, the participant's stated reason, and the exact posts it reacted to, so every claimed influence can be traced back to its source.

## 9. Social diffusion graph

- **Voiceover:** The diffusion view draws the simulation as a graph of audience groups and their posts, round by round, and the feed browser filters every post by round and by group. The view is labeled as illustrative. It is not a forecast of real reach.

## 10. Participant memory and follow-ups

*Answers cite stored memories and become memories*

- **Recording:** `08-follow-ups.mp4`
- **Real time:** 2 min 12 s; shown at 5.58x
- **Voiceover:** Participants keep memory. This participant's current stance comes from round one, and its memories are its interview and its own post. We ask a follow-up about carrying the empty can back down. The answer cites one stored memory and is saved as a new memory. We can also question a whole group. A seeded sample of five answered, and the tally covers only that sample.

## 11. Risk Radar with vision

*Observations kept apart from interpretations*

- **Recording:** `10-risk-radar.mp4`
- **Real time:** 2 min 8 s; shown at 3.64x
- **Voiceover:** Risk Radar scans the copy and the image together. A vision model, MiniCPM-V, analyzed the uploaded creative. In twenty-seven seconds the scan found five potential issues, one moderate and four low. The moderate one is the line, crack one open at the summit, which the model reads as a possible clash with etiquette at sacred peaks in Japan. That reading is labeled as model interpretation. Applying the suggested change creates a new adaptation. The campaign itself is never edited in place.

## 12. Voice adaptations

Each change shows what it replaces, why, and whether evidence supports it, and deterministic checks confirm that protected terms were kept and prohibited terms avoided.

## 13. Loop evaluation

*Deterministic decision rules, no invented winner*

- **Recording:** `12-loop-evaluation.mp4`
- **Real time:** 0 min 44 s; shown at 2.32x
- **Voiceover:** Loop scores the original and three candidates under one scoring version. The best candidate scored ninety point five against eighty-five point eight. Because the margin is under five points, the decision rule recommends validating with real people instead of declaring a winner.

## 14. Versions on identical participants

*Same 100-person population, version 2 against version 3*

- **Recording:** `09-version-rerun.mp4, 13-version-comparison.mp4`
- **Real time:** 1 min 26 s; shown at 3.0x
- **Voiceover:** Loop can also compare versions on identical participants. We saved the risk fix as version three and interviewed the same one hundred participants again. Reusing the population skipped all Qloo and construction work. The result is honest: positive reactions fell from seventy-one to sixty-six percent. The fix may remove a cultural risk but cost emotional pull, and the app states that no statistical significance is claimed.

## 15. Cancel and recovery

*A run stops at a checkpoint and keeps its finished work*

- **Recording:** `21-cancel-resume.mp4`
- **Real time:** 8 min 20 s; shown at 14.06x
- **Voiceover:** Long runs can be cancelled. We start another study on the same population and cancel it after the first batch. It stops at the next checkpoint and keeps what was finished: forty of one hundred interviews, saved and readable. A cancelled run is final. Resume is for runs that failed or were interrupted, for example by a server restart. When we restarted the server during the next run, Render kept the old instance running until the new one was healthy, so that run simply completed. Recovery after a hard stop is covered by the automated tests.
